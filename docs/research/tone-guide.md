# Tone Guide: Ironic Corporate Ad-Speak

**Status:** Binding content standard for all preset copy (AGENTS.md §2, digital-wellbeing.md §3).
Every copy line shipped in a preset must instantiate an approved pattern from this
document or carry an explicit reviewed exception.

## 1. The Voice

Retarget talks like the world's most wholesome advertising agency. The client is
"fresh air." The product is your own wellbeing. The copy sounds like a brand
campaign — slogan cadence, confident simplicity, the occasional absurd boast —
because that register is what decades of ad exposure taught your brain to find
compelling. The irony is the transparency mechanism: the user *knows* it's an ad
parody, is in on the joke, and the message lands anyway. Being in on the joke
lowers resistance to persuasion (reactance is reduced when the persuasive intent
is disclosed and owned — see digital-wellbeing.md §3, transparency principle).

The target feeling after reading a line: a small smile and *"okay, fine, I'll
drink some water."* Never guilt. Never obligation. Never "should."

## 2. Copy Constraints (hard limits)

- **Length:** ≤ 42 characters preferred for notification copy, hard cap 64.
  Interruption-timing.md §4: copy is short; the image does the persuading.
- **Valence:** Positive only. digital-wellbeing.md §3 rules 1, 2, 10 bind:
  no manufactured urgency, no loss framing, no fear appeals, no shame.
- **Sensory concreteness:** Prefer concrete sensory nouns and verbs over abstractions
  ("crisp," "cold," "sun-warmed") — advertising-psychology.md §6: concrete,
  multi-sense encoding beats abstract messaging.
- **No fake claims, no real ones either:** We never assert health outcomes. Water is
  delicious and wet; that's as far as it goes.
- **English-only in v1**, strings externalized (Android standards, AGENTS.md §4).

## 3. Approved Patterns

Each pattern has: the mechanism it exploits (with research citation), the template,
and example lines. Examples are illustrative — the pattern is the contract, the
examples show its range.

### 3.1 Minimalist Confidence (brand-slogan cadence)
**Mechanism:** Mere exposure + low-effort processing. Short declaratives process
fluently; fluency feels true. (advertising-psychology.md §1, §3b peripheral route)
**Template:** `[Concrete noun]. [Two-to-five word payoff].`
- "Water. It's what plants crave."
- "Sunlight. Free range. Free."
- "Apples. Nature's original snack food."

### 3.2 Absurd Boast (parody ad claim)
**Mechanism:** Humor increases ad likability and recall (humor effect); the
self-aware absurdity signals parody, keeping the disclosure honest.
**Template:** `[Product] : now with [unexpected upgrade/ad-speak flourish].`
- "Fresh air: now with more outdoors."
- "Hydration: featuring award-winning wetness."
- "Vegetables: redesigned by 400 million years of R&D."

### 3.3 Luxury Repositioning (premium ad-speak for the mundane)
**Mechanism:** Evaluative conditioning — pairing the behavior with prestige
affect rubs off on the behavior. (advertising-psychology.md §2)
**Template:** `A truly [premium adjective] [experience word]. [Behavior reframed as indulgence].`
- "A truly artisanal glass of water."
- "Sunlight. The limited edition is daily."
- "Bespoke produce, hand-grown by the Earth™."

### 3.4 Corporate Synergy (meaningless-business-speak parody)
**Mechanism:** Full parody register — maximal irony distance; the joke IS the
corporate emptiness, so the persuasive intent stays fully disclosed.
**Template:** `[Behavior], but make it [fashionable modifier].`
- "Going outside, but make it wellness."
- "Vegetables: leveraging synergistic crunch verticals."
- "Hydration solutions for the modern lifestyle."

### 3.5 Call-to-Action Parody (ad CTA, redirected at you)
**Mechanism:** Ironic imperative works as an implementation-intention cue — a
*when-then trigger* dressed as an ad. (behavior-change-science.md: implementation
intentions; interruption-timing.md §4 tailoring)
**Template:** `[Imperative]. [Reassuring/minimal effort addendum].`
- "Go outside. Briefly. We'll take it."
- "Drink water now-ish. No pressure."
- "Eat a vegetable today. Start anywhere."

### 3.6 Sensory Close-Up (food/still-life ad style)
**Mechanism:** Visual superiority + sensory words deepen encoding even in text
channels. (advertising-psychology.md §6)
**Template:** `[Sensory adjective] [noun], [sensory verb phrase].`
- "Cold water. Satisfyingly loud when it hits the glass."
- "Crisp apples, crunching at volume."
- "Warm sun on forearms. Apply generously."

### 3.7 Heritage/Nostalgia Brand (established-brand parody)
**Mechanism:** Nostalgia is a reliable positive-affect shortcut; frames the
behavior as classic/trusted.
**Template:** `[Behavior]: trusted by humans since [absurd early date].`
- "Walking outdoors: trusted by humans since 300,000 BCE."
- "Fruit: a family tradition, generations strong."
- "Hydration: a classic for a reason."

### 3.8 New Product Launch (tech-ad parody)
**Mechanism:** Novelty framing (novelty draws attention in peripheral processing)
directed at something ancient — the gap is the joke.
**Template:** `[Introducing/The all-new] [behavior] — [tech-ad flourish].`
- "Introducing: lunch outside. Now in stunning daylight."
- "The all-new apple. No subscription required."
- "Announcing water 2.0. Same great taste. Zero calories."

### 3.9 Testimonial Parody (satisfied-customer ad style)
**Mechanism:** Social proof structure made honest — the satisfied customer is you,
in the near future; self-congruence positioning. (advertising-psychology.md §5)
**Template:** `"[Quote from ideal-self, post-behavior]." — You, soon`
- "I went outside and my mood noticed." — You, soon
- "Best water I've had all day." — You, in a minute
- "Turns out I like fruit." — You, eventually

### 3.10 Fine Print Joke (small-print ad parody)
**Mechanism:** The small print is where ads hide the truth; ours hides another
kind encouragement. Subverts the darkest ad convention into warmth.
**Template:** `[Main line]. *[Fine print: small kind act or reassurance]*.`
- "Drink water. *Contains 0% regrets.*"
- "Go outside for a bit. *Offer valid whenever.*"
- "Eat a peach. *Side effects include drippy chin. Worth it.*"

### 3.11 Sponsorship Parody (event/media sponsorship style)
**Mechanism:** Sponsors attach their brand to wholesome moments; we sponsor the
user's own life moments. Borrowed-frame, honest owner.
**Template:** `[Moment] brought to you by [behavior].`
- "This slump is brought to you by mild dehydration. Fixable."
- "Golden hour, proudly sponsored by the sun."
- "Your walk today is brought to you by your legs."

### 3.12 Mascot Wisdom (trusted-brand-character parody)
**Mechanism:** Brand characters deliver repeated exposure with likability and
memorability. Ours is a generic wise/cheeky presence with no persona overhead.
**Template:** `[Nature-object pronouncement], lightly smug.`
- "The river has never once missed a day."
- "Trees recommend this product."
- "The ocean remains very hydrated, FYI."

### 3.13 Underpromise (responsible-ad parody)
**Mechanism:** Anti-hype inversion of exaggerated ad claims; manages expectations
while staying positive-valence. Reactance-safe by design.
**Template:** `[Behavior]. [Small, honest, weirdly satisfying claim].`
- "Water: probably helps."
- "Going for a walk: mildly heroic."
- "A banana. It's not going to change your life. It's a banana."

### 3.14 Lifestyle Integration (lifestyle-ad parody)
**Mechanism:** Ads sell contexts-of-use, not products; we attach the behavior to
the user's real contexts (desk, commute, couch). Contextual tailoring boost.
(interruption-timing.md §4)
**Template:** `[Your everyday context] + [the behavior as an upgrade to it].`
- "Desk life. Now compatible with hydration."
- "The 3 PM slump has a plant-based solution."
- "Commute upgrade available: sky."

### 3.15 Elegant Minimalism (high-end-brand deadpan)
**Mechanism:** Luxury minimalism registers as confidence; confidence persuades
peripherally. Pairs with photography-first channels like wallpaper.
**Template:** `[One word or phrase]. [Optional: fragment. Nothing else.]`
- "Hydrate."
- "Outside. Again."
- "Green things. Eat some."

### 3.16 As-Seen-On-TV (direct-response parody)
**Mechanism:** DRTV cadence is instantly recognizable and deeply funny as parody;
the "call to action" is trivially completable, converting parody to action.
**Template:** `[How-to simplicity]. [Bonus flourish]. [Tiny "order now" parody].`
- "Step 1: fruit. Step 2: enjoy. That's it. That's the ad."
- "Just add water. To yourself. Act today."
- "Walk 300 steps. Operators standing by. (It's you.)"

### 3.17 Comparative Ad (competitor parody)
**Mechanism:** The "competitor" is the sedentary status quo or the processed
alternative, framed not as evil but as simply less cool.
**Template:** `[Behavior] vs. [status quo]: [verdict].`
- "Tap water vs. dry mouth. A tale of two lifestyles."
- "Verdict's in: outside air rated fresher than inside air."
- "Fruit: the other fast food."

### 3.18 Seasonal Campaign (holiday-ad parody)
**Mechanism:** Seasonal framing piggybacks on temporal landmarks — the fresh-start
effect — dressing goal-relevant moments as retail seasons.
**Template:** `[Season/moment] [sale-speak]. [Behavior as the limited offer].`
- "Fall special: crisp air, limited-time foliage."
- "Summer's hottest drop: sunshine."
- "The Monday sale: everything outside is free again."

### 3.19 Infomercial Problem-Solution (before/after parody)
**Mechanism:** Classic problem-agitate-solve ad structure, defanged — the "problem"
is trivial, the solution immediate. Solves the fear-appeal ban: no distress, only
playful recognition.
**Template:** `[Tiny everyday friction]. [Instantly available fix].`
- "Tired of being thirsty? So is your body. Water exists."
- "Craving crunch? Carrots heard you."
- "Can't focus? The park has a money-back guarantee."

### 3.20 Milestone Celebration (loyalty-program parody)
**Mechanism:** Progress celebration without punitive streak mechanics. Unlike
guilt-streaks, every-count-is-a-win framing. (digital-wellbeing.md §3 rule 2:
no post-lapse guilt; fresh-start framing)
**Template:** `[Small number/trivial achievement] recognized as [absurd grandeur].`
- "Day 1 of drinking water. Champagne for you. Or, water."
- "You've unlocked: outside. Rewards program: feelings."
- "Congratulations on the cucumber. Legend status pending."

## 4. Anti-Patterns (reject in review, no exceptions without maintainer sign-off)

| Anti-pattern | Why banned | Violates |
|---|---|---|
| Guilt/shame ("another day skipped...") | Post-lapse distress, reactance | dwb §3.2 |
| Fake urgency ("only 2 hours left!") | Manufactured scarcity | dwb §3.1 |
| Countdown/loss framing ("don't break it") | Loss aversion weaponized | dwb §3.1–2 |
| Fear appeals ("dehydration dangers") | Drives avoidance, not adoption | dwb §3.10 |
| Snark that punches down ("still on the couch?") | Mockery of the user, not the genre | §1 in-joke rule |
| Earnest lecture mode ("hydration is important") | Loses the voice; central-route lecturing in peripheral channels | psych §3b |
| Health claims ("boosts immunity") | Medical claim, legal + trust risk | §2 no-claims |
| Real brand mimicry ("Just Do It" clones) | Trademark + lazy; punch at genre, not brands | §5 |
| Engagement bait ("tap to see more!") | Attention-maximizing pattern | dwb §3.6 |

## 5. Validation (enforcement in code)

`PresetCatalogTest` rejects any copy line that:

1. Exceeds 64 characters (hard cap; warn ≥ 43).
2. Contains an anti-pattern keyword heuristics list (current starter list:
   "should", "don't break", "only ... left", "last chance", "limited time",
   "danger", "risk of", "guilty").
3. Has no `tonePattern` field referencing a pattern in this guide (§3.1–3.20).
4. Has no `citation` field pointing at a docs/research/ file + section.

Keyword lists are heuristic tripwires, not censorship — flagged lines get human
review, not auto-deletion. The validator also enforces: each preset covers ≥ 4
distinct patterns (fatigue-aware rotation needs thematic variety —
advertising-psychology.md §4: rotating themes delays wearout).

## 6. Living Document

New patterns require: mechanism + citation, template, ≥ 2 example lines, and a
check against the anti-pattern table. Retired patterns keep their section (marked
"retired") so existing preset copy doesn't silently orphan.
